# Services Copy v01: three audiences, one offer

**Produced through:** workflows/11-services-copy.md
**Date:** 2026-07-02. Status: draft, awaiting Demi's read-aloud and statement picks.
**The offer, fixed:** 01 CONCEPT · 02 PROOF · 03 CAMPAIGN. The voice never changes; the need does.

---

## Variant A: Architects

The meeting to win: the approval room (client, board, jury) where the design must be judged as designed.

### Statement (pick one)
1. **Images your design can be judged on.** (Recommended: falsifiable, the approval-room need in six words, only a studio doing design-intent work says "judged".)
2. From competition board to signed-off design.
3. We draw the part the drawings can't say.

### Stage ledes
- **01 Concept** — A scheme made legible before it is fully drawn. For the competition, the client, the committee that decides if it lives.
- **02 Proof** — Materials, light, junctions. Frames precise enough to approve, or to argue with; both are wins.
- **03 Campaign** — The built story, told once and reused: publication, awards, the practice's own record.

### Harry Dry notes
- "or to argue with; both are wins" pictures how architects actually use renders in review. Only a studio that has sat through revision rounds says it.
- Never claim design contribution: "we draw the part the drawings can't say" walks the line; if it reads as authorship to Demi, drop option 3 entirely.

## Variant B: Developers

The meeting to win: the investor table first, the sales launch second.

### Statement (pick one)
1. **Your project, sold before it breaks ground.** (Recommended: the entire developer need in one falsifiable line.)
2. From investor deck to sales gallery, one image world.
3. Believability, on the schedule your capital works to.

### Stage ledes
- **01 Concept** — The project made believable while it is still a number on a term sheet. For lenders, partners, approvals.
- **02 Proof** — One set of frames the whole room decides on: unit, amenity, view. Fewer meetings, faster yes.
- **03 Campaign** — Film, stills, and social cut from the same world, ready for the gallery, the site, and the feed on launch day.

### Harry Dry notes
- "still a number on a term sheet" passes picture-it for this audience specifically; an architect wouldn't say it, which is the point.
- No speed promises with numbers (operational, not headline); "on the schedule your capital works to" gestures at speed without a claim we must defend.

---

## Variant C: Technical Directors

The meeting to win: the capacity decision, made by someone who could do the work in-house and needs a partner who will not lower the bar.

### Statement (pick one)
1. **Capacity that holds your standard.** (Recommended: four words, the whole TD anxiety, falsifiable against delivery history.)
2. The overflow partner your own team signs off on.
3. Trusted by the people who could do it themselves.

### Stage ledes
- **01 Concept** — Early narrative your team can hand off whole: brief in, direction back, no hand-holding in between.
- **02 Proof** — Comments picked up the first time. Frames that pass your internal QA, not just the client's.
- **03 Campaign** — Finals delivered to spec, on the date, in every format the rollout needs.

### Harry Dry notes
- Option 3 reuses the live peer-band line; if both appear on the same page it repeats, pick one surface for it.
- "Comments picked up the first time" is the sharpest falsifiable line in the set and speaks directly to how TDs score vendors. It is also a standard the studio must actually hold (the Bally's lesson); shipping this line is a commitment, flag to Demi.

## Acceptance sweep

Zero em dashes. Banned words: absent (checked; no premium/luxury/bespoke/seamless/innovative family). No brand yardsticks. No pricing talk. No design-contribution claims (option A3 flagged for Demi's judgment). No US-office implication. English throughout.

## The interchangeability test (quality bar)

Shown the wrong variant: a TD shrugs at "sold before it breaks ground" (not their job), a developer shrugs at "comments picked up the first time" (never reads comments), an architect shrugs at "capacity that holds your standard" (has no overflow problem). Passes.
